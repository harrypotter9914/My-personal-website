Natural-Interaction VR Action Adventure

Puzzlehand

A controller-free VR action-adventure that turns the player's hands into the movement, climbing, magic, combat, and puzzle interface. Built as my Bachelor of Engineering thesis, the project combines Unity, HTC Vive, and Noitom Hi5 2.0 gloves in three progressively layered levels.

01 / Project Intent

Remove the controller and make physical gestures the game's core language.

Puzzlehand explores how a VR game changes when grabbing, pulling, climbing, casting, and operating machinery are performed through tracked hands instead of handheld controllers. The goal was not to add gesture input on top of a conventional game, but to design traversal and puzzles around the affordances of natural hand movement.

I designed and programmed the gameplay, interaction systems, and three-level progression. Visual and audio assets were third-party or production resources; they are not presented as my original art.

Core design question

Can one consistent hand-based interaction language support locomotion, traversal, combat, resource decisions, and environmental puzzles without relying on a controller?

02 / Interaction Systems

Every major verb begins with the player's hands.

Hand-drag Locomotion

Players press and drag against the virtual ground to propel themselves. Motion thresholds, direction sampling, gravity, and inertia smoothing translate hand movement into stable traversal.

Two-hand Climbing

Grabbable surfaces switch the character between grounded and climbing states. Releasing either hand transfers tracked displacement into body movement while collision logic prevents invalid motion.

Magic, Combat & Resources

Gesture-cast abilities connect ranged interaction, enemy pressure, and environmental problem solving. Resource limits make magic a traversal and planning decision rather than unlimited spectacle.

Physical Mechanisms

Levers, buttons, rotating cranks, keycard validators, and joystick-style controls reuse consistent grabbing, collision, rotation, and state-change rules.

03 / Level Design

Three levels teach, combine, and pressure-test the interaction vocabulary.

04 / Technical Implementation

Modular controllers keep gesture data, player state, collision, and feedback readable.

The project separates tracking input from gameplay state changes. Hand position and gesture data feed movement and interaction controllers; those controllers then coordinate gravity, CharacterController collision, climbing state, object constraints, and visual feedback.

This structure made the same low-level grabbing and motion rules reusable across traversal, rotating mechanisms, validators, and combat interactions, while allowing each object to expose its own state and completion logic.

Implementation priorities

Stable movement, reusable interaction components, visible state changes, and hardware calibration that could be tested one behavior at a time.

05 / Hardware Validation

Each interaction was calibrated and tested on the target hardware.

Input Setup

Noitom Hi5 2.0 gloves provided tracked hand input while HTC Vive supplied the headset and VR environment. Calibration aligned the physical hands, avatar hands, and interaction volumes.

Functional Testing

Climbing, cranks, levers, buttons, validators, and joystick interactions were tested independently before being combined inside full level routes.

Iteration

Thresholds, collision ranges, release behavior, and mechanism constraints were tuned to reduce accidental activation and make intended hand motion easier to read.

06 / Outcome

A thesis-scale project that connects interaction research to a complete playable progression.

Puzzlehand demonstrates my strongest working pattern: define a player-facing design question, build the underlying system, author content that teaches it, and validate the result on real hardware. The final prototype supports controller-free movement, climbing, magic, combat, physical mechanisms, and three escalating levels within one coherent gesture language.
